How it works

  1. Write something. Type a word, or paste ASCII art.
  2. It becomes a grid. A word is first drawn in a small 5×7 pixel font. With ASCII art, every character that isn't a space becomes a cell.
  3. Rows are pitch, columns are time. The top line is the highest note. Each column is one step, a 16th note. Characters side by side join into one held note when Sustain runs is on.
  4. Rows follow a scale. Pentatonic is the default, so any shape sounds musical. Try major, minor, blues or chromatic, and pick a key.
  5. Press Play. The letters fall into place, the notes form, a 1-2-3-4 count-in plays, and the roll is performed on the instrument you chose.
  6. Add more if you like. Turn on drums, change the tempo, or switch on Loop. You can keep typing while it plays and the music changes as you go.

Tips: lines that slope make melodies, tall columns make chords, and gaps make silence. Your name has a tune too.

About musiciate

Every word has a shape. Every shape has a rhythm. musiciate is a way of listening to both.

Language, drawing and music are three of the oldest ways we have to say what we feel. We usually keep them apart: words on the page, pictures on the wall, sound in the air. Here they share a single grid. The letters you type become a picture, and the picture becomes a melody.

Nothing is composed and nothing is added. It is only translated. A heart doesn't sound like a star. A name doesn't sound like a question. The music was already there, waiting in the form.

It's a small reminder that expression is one thing wearing different clothes. Meaning moves freely between what we say, what we draw and what we hear.

musiciate is a project by Boston Sense, part of the Sense Labs experiments: small tools that model how art, music, language and expression flow into one another.
